\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}}

This paper asks whether two structurally different problems---selection of Standard-Model structure and the relation
between quantum and gravitational descriptions---can be studied with one finite grammar of quotients, closures,
obstructions, and common refinements. The first problem includes the gauge algebra
\(\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)\), representations, generations, scales, and vacuum
data~\citep{Weinberg1967}. The second includes perturbative non-renormalizability and the absence of an experimentally
confirmed quantum theory of gravity~\citep{tHooftVeltman1974,GoroffSagnotti1985}. A shared vocabulary is not itself a
solution; it earns evidential weight only where explicit finite constructions and can-fail controls survive review.

This paper instantiates SBT's finite obstruction, quotient, construction-audit, and claim-governance subset. It does not
instantiate SBT's strict-extension, reclosure, promotion, endogenous-repair, or run-level dynamical machinery, and it
supplies no substrate-specific continuum bridge. The main contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item
  \textbf{A finite Standard-Model selection construction} (Section 4). A conjugation-consistent carrier contains
  \(1{,}066\) labelled genuinely-chiral structures, \(419\)
  declared quotient orbits, or \(195\) primitive-normalized orbits. Under the declared representation, charge, field,
  and scalar caps, clean separation uniquely selects a \emph{breaking-orientation branch class}: \(2|3\) alone admits
  clean branches, all of them \(SU(2)\)-active, while every \(SU(4)\) branch is breaking. This is not bare-structure
  uniqueness because every \(2|3\) structure also has a breaking branch. A separate witness-independent theorem excludes
  stable single-factor \(SU(N)\) candidates in its stated toy grammar.
\item
  \textbf{Constructed unification objects and a content-orbit result} (Section 4). Given the regular \(SU(5)\) embedding,
  the \(\overline{\mathbf5}+\mathbf{10}\) package, and a weak-pair convention, exact matrices force a one-dimensional
  hypercharge direction and yield \(\sin^2\theta_W=3/8\) and \(k_Y=5/3\). Generator action computes the \(X/Y\)
  roles inside that frame. The ratios do not select the parent: Pati--Salam also gives \(3/8\). Under the declared
  content quotient, the candidate SM/alternative pair
  is one orbit; content is unique only in this recognition template.
\item
  \textbf{A conditional QM--GR access construction and finite-graph LP recognition} (Section 5). On an abstract Boolean
  carrier, the declared QM and GR coordinate partitions are mutually non-factorizing. A separate 54-record
  field-plus-background population realizes conditional, coarse readouts with explicit phase and potential controls;
  this is finite provenance, not type or instance uniqueness. On a graph carrier, independently contracted-state
  entropies, exact cuts, a fair GHZ control, and exported LP certificates support the identity
  \(\operatorname{Area}(\mathrm{mincut})=\sum_e c_e y_e\), with \(y_e\) edge shadow prices. An exact computation also
  shows that the paper's two conditional-mean completions --- the natural ``quantize'' and ``curve'' routes on this
  carrier --- commute, so no route-mismatch explanation of quantum gravity's difficulty is available at this level.
\item
  \textbf{Three research programs, with two P1 finite subproblems completed} (Section 6). At graph level, a completed
  finite computation establishes
  forward-reachability disjointness for nineteen pairs, while symmetric-closure orbit disjointness remains open. At state
  level, six exact joint-map fibers establish noninjectivity but collapse under a constructed exact diagonal tensor
  gauge. The other two programs remain open: whether
  a perfect-record gravitational channel is dynamically derived, and which bounded scalar-dressed invariant candidates
  persist as physical records. Each section distinguishes a finite falsifiable statement, where one exists, from the
  unsupported universal claim.
\item
  \textbf{Narrow breadth results and two toy theorems} (Sections 4 and 7). The L* layer is a coherent finite
  construction with a one-layer and block-structure caveat. The single-factor result and the exact abstract
  coordinate-partition incomparability result are genuine toy theorems. Other breadth claims carry only their stated
  finite or contested grades; none establishes universality.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Scope.} Nothing here derives nature-level Standard-Model parameters, solves quantum gravity, proves a
universal composition law, or transfers from these carriers to physical systems. ``Recognition'' means that an exact
known structure is instantiated after all named imports are declared. ``Open program'' means that a construction is
identified as missing and the repository isolates exactly what would have to be built.

\textbf{Reader's map.} Section 2 gives the calculus vocabulary; Section 3 the audit method; Sections 4--5 the
track results; Section 6
