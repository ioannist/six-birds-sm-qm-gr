# Cross-track comparison: one vocabulary, conditional recurrences

The two tracks use the same formal vocabulary: quotients, closure, factorization defects, common refinements, and typed
landing modes. That reuse is real and useful for organizing finite constructions. Post-publication review does not,
however, support the stronger Version-1 statements that this grammar “forces” cross-domain physics, achieves a
foundational grade, or demonstrates universality. Several proposed ties depended on claims now retracted: record-stability
grounding, a forced gravitational record channel, route non-commutation, and one Born–area composition law.

## The anti-contamination protocol and its limit

The SM construction was typed as a selection/measure layer and the QM–GR construction as a common-refinement/access
problem. Validators rejected certain cross-track tokens, and the build history contains genuine self-demotions. This is
evidence of procedural separation, not mathematical independence or blinding: the protocol was designed and reported by
the same program, the external missing-layer gate never closed, and the atlases are not independent preregistration
evidence. Hence recurrence of a formal object across the tracks is a comparison datum, not proof that nature realizes a
substrate-independent law.

The table supports a modest statement: the same typed questions can expose analogous failure modes on two deliberately
different finite substrates. It does not identify the physical mechanisms. In particular:

- the $SU(5)$ $X/Y$ coset has several computed roles inside one constructed frame, but it is not the QM--GR
   factorization defect and does not explain perturbative non-renormalizability;
- record candidates in the gauge carrier and a classical-record quantum channel share terminology, but no cross-layer
   map connects them and neither forces the other;
- F51-shaped commuting diagrams organize both an embedding and an access fork, but this formal resemblance does not
   select $SU(5)$, prove a unique QM–GR parent, or establish a universal composition law.

## The bounded one-grammar statement

The certified cross-cutting claim is therefore:

> One frozen structural vocabulary was instantiated on two finite, differently typed carriers. It produced several sound
> conditional constructions, two genuine toy theorems, and useful diagnostics of where stronger claims failed. The
> recurrence is an $n=2$ methodological datum. It neither demonstrates universality nor transfers any statement to
> nature.

This bound is substantive. It makes the failed predictions informative rather than hiding them: exact quotienting turns
the published P1 kernel into gauge, while P1's graph- and state-level finite routes are resolved within their declared
relations by nineteen exact graph fibers and six finite connected $C2_L1$ examples; explicit channel analysis isolates
P2's missing dynamical premise; and a broader invariant census shows P3's dependence on record definition. P1's broader
physical and bulk-geometry interpretation remains open, and P2 and P3 remain construction programs requiring
gravitational dynamics and invariant-ring/dynamical record machinery not supplied here.

## Limits

- Both carrier families are finite and deliberately capped; there is no continuum or nature-level frame transfer.
- The anti-contamination guard is procedural self-report, not independent blinding.
- Recognition results retain every named import: in particular the regular $SU(5)$ embedding and the graph/tensor
   network model.
- The campaign completed an adversarial claims pass and produced machine-verifiable repairs; it is not journal review
   or independent replication.
- The missing-layer and unification atlases have unresolved public-support limitations listed in Section 9 and
   Appendix B.
