## 0. The method point: judge each track on its own bar, not the reductionist one

A foundational contribution is judged by five things: (1) does it **locate** a real, deep problem precisely; (2) does
it offer a **principled, genuinely new resolution**; (3) does the **structure cohere / close**; (4) does it **unify or
recover** things previously separate; (5) does it yield **checkable consequences and honest limits**.

The trap — the one SBT's own anti-reductionism primer warns against — is to swap criterion (2)/(5) for a *textbook
deliverable the track never set out to produce*, then score the absence as failure. Each track has a **different wrong
bar**:

- **QM-GR's wrong bar: "produce gravitons / a quantum metric."** Gravitons are the unconfirmed deliverable of the
  *quantize-the-geometry* program — exactly the program QM-GR's result shows does not close. Demanding them is scoring
  the framework by the wishlist of the approach it structurally supersedes. (Special relativity, in 1905, had no new
  data and no collider; it was judged great on structure.)
- **SM's wrong bar: "derive the SM's parameters."** By SBT's *own* laws (F47 fine-tuning, F26 moduli/contingency) the
  SM's constants are **predicted** to be contingent selections on which no value-law can land. Demanding their
  derivation is demanding the framework violate its own correct prediction. The right analog is **Galois theory**:
  judging it by "give me a formula for the quintic's roots" misses that the achievement *is* proving no such formula
  exists and classifying exactly what is solvable.

So the two tracks are **two different genres** of foundational contribution — a *unification/reframe* and a
*demarcation/selection-rule* result — and each is assessed below on its own genre's bar.
