\section*{Bounded Grade}

This is a finite-carrier diagnostic on the Cluster-A selection toy. It tests whether the Step-8 structural survivor distribution over seven facets factorizes into independent per-facet selections, or whether it carries cross-facet coupling. It is not a claim about the real physical layer count or any physical value.

\section*{Setup}

The facets are
\[
X=(G,R,N,T,E,U,V),
\]
represented in the artifacts as \texttt{gauge\_code}, \texttt{rep\_code}, \texttt{n\_gen}, \texttt{texture\_code}, \texttt{ew\_code}, \texttt{uv\_code}, and \texttt{vacuum\_code}. The neutral input is the Step-8 product carrier of 8640 worlds. The tested distribution is the Step-8 structural survivor set of 468 worlds.

The total correlation is
\[
C(X)=\sum_i H(X_i)-H(X_1,\ldots,X_7),
\]
computed empirically in bits. The neutral product is the can-fail control: it must give \(C \approx 0\).

The neutral product has zero total correlation at numerical precision. The structural survivors have positive total correlation, so the Step-8 structural constraints induce dependence across the facets.

\section*{Block Decomposition}

At the declared threshold of 0.01 bits, the main survivor coupling graph has two connected components:
\[
\{G,N,R,T,U,V\} \quad \text{and} \quad \{E\}.
\]
In artifact names, this is
\[
\texttt{gauge\_code+n\_gen+rep\_code+texture\_code+uv\_code+vacuum\_code}
\quad / \quad
\texttt{ew\_code}.
\]
The within-block correlation is 2.193305564583 bits. The residual coupling lost by the block-product approximation is 0.035998916880 bits.

\section*{Verdict}

The test does not support a fully separable set of independent per-facet selections: the survivor total correlation is positive. It also does not support a clean all-facet single component on the main survivor graph. The honest finite-toy verdict is block-structured coupling: a six-facet coupled block plus a weakly separate EW facet, with a small residual cross-block coupling.

This narrows the one-layer reading. The toy gives internal support for coupled selection structure, but it does not prove a single physical selection layer.
