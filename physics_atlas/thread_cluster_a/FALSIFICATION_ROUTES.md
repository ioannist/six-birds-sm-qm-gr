# SBT → physics: falsification routes (making the framework checkable)

> **Purpose.** A menu of routes to **falsifiable claims** — the bridge from "lawful strict extension (proven new
> structure)" to "a prediction a physicist can test." Crystallized from the SM-track discussion; the routes are
> **framework-general** (apply to SBT broadly), not specific to Cluster A. Ranked by tractability, with the honest
> obstacle for each treated as the *door where the work happens*, not a wall.
>
> **The core tension to keep in view.** SBT's strongest asset — **proven blindness** (rigorously proving the
> framework *cannot* determine X) — works *against* "derive-a-constant" falsifiability: every blindness theorem
> removes a constant from the predictable set. So the framework's **natural falsifiable home is structural /
> relational + emergence-transition claims**, where the strict-extension property (lawful, forced, new structure)
> *becomes* the checkable consequence — not numerical derivation, except where a quantity is provably *not* blind.

---

## Route 1 — Derived constant (gold standard; strongest, narrowest, hardest)

Run the six birds **for real** (an E0 emergence run on an actual substrate) and read off an SM number; it matches
measurement or it doesn't. Two constraints decide whether it's a *derivation* (falsifiable) or a *fit* (not):

1. **Blindness gating.** The derivable set = constants the framework does **not** prove blindness to. On the SM,
   that set came out nearly empty (content, N_gen, masses are all *proven blind*; what's left is mostly known
   structural ratios like `sin²θ_W=3/8`). **First step is not "run E0" — it's "find a quantity the framework can
   see."**
2. **Substrate-independence.** The number must come with a **non-definability / forcing proof that it is
   substrate-forced, not tuned** (the Cantor-style strict-extension argument, applied to a *number* instead of to
   structure). Without that, a different substrate/tuning gives a different number → a fit, not a prediction.

A single new such number that matched would be the whole game. Hardest route precisely because the framework is
honest about its blindness.

## Route 2 — F-law falsification (most physicist-ready)

Foundations IV = 52 layer-agnostic structural laws, each of the form *"any system meeting hypotheses H must exhibit
conclusion Z."* That is directly falsifiable: name a **real system that meets H but violates Z** → the law dies.
The checkable claim: *"real system X instantiates F-law Y, therefore it must show Z."* Pick a law (F27
conservation/orbit-descent; F48 gluing/topology; F37 non-joint-access/complementarity), name a real system, derive
the checkable Z. Ready content because the laws **claim universality**. (Caveat: the laws are theorems in the formal
setting — the falsifiable part is whether real physics is in their *domain*; that's the claim to expose.)

## Route 3 — Tractable substrate (the realistic FIRST test)

Don't use the SM (E0 run excluded as a ~100k-LOC simulation; blindness maximal). Run the six birds **for real** on a
**small system where emergence is computable** (a stat-mech model, active matter, a network, a quantum-information
setup — the `To Create / Plot / Chart a Stone` substrates) and predict a **new measurable**: a phase transition, a
scaling exponent, a thermodynamic bound. A clean, checkable prediction on a runnable system beats a conditional SM
number and is reachable now.

## Route 4 — Thermodynamic consequence (the Cantor template)

The Cantor paper's 4th theoremlet shows a **strict extension emits a thermodynamic consequence** (conditional
pressure disintegration / closure-deficit) — and thermodynamic consequences are **measurable**. So: strict-extend a
real system → derive its closure-deficit / an entropy-production or fluctuation bound → measure it. This is the
framework's most *natural* bridge from "proven new structure" to "checkable number," because the machinery already
produces such consequences.

## Route 5 — Forbidden co-occurrence (non-factorization)

`Δ_fact` predicts certain features are **one object** — must co-occur or cannot. A falsifiable version is a
structural **exclusion not already predicted by standard physics**: *"A and B cannot be decoupled."* If observed
decoupled → falsified. (The X/Y-coset unification — color-separation + proton-stability + no-monopole = one object
— is the template, but its *surface* claim overlaps GUTs; the **new exclusion** is the record-stability **link** —
see the Step-69 worked instance below.)

## Worked instance — E032 (measurement): the D-vs-Σ_f discriminator (already located)

The most developed falsifiable claim in the atlas, and a concrete case of Routes 2/5. SBT locates the measurement
problem's residue as the **single-outcome selection predicate** — proven non-definable from QM's Σ_f by an explicit
non-factorization witness (two global states with identical diagonal density matrix + Born weights, split by "which
outcome is the fact") — and emits a sharp **out-of-sample structural prediction** about *where in the closure
package (Z, f, Σ_f, E, D)* any future single-outcome theory must touch:

> Any future single-outcome (measurement-selection) theory must **either modify QM's defect-ledger D** (add a
> staging parameter / non-unitary term → objective-collapse family, e.g. CSL's noise field, GRW rate) **or modify
> only the readout Σ_f** (add an indexical/self-locating predicate, no new dynamics → many-worlds family). A layer
> touching **D** is collapse-type; a layer touching **only Σ_f** is MWI-type.

**Falsifier:** a community-accepted single-outcome theory that closes the gap with QM's closure package **literally
unchanged** — touching *neither* D nor Σ_f — would **falsify** the non-descending-object claim (selection would
have been definable from below after all). The card stakes its standing risk on exactly this. It is a *no-fitting*
prediction: it says where any future theory MUST and MUST NOT touch, *before* such a theory exists. **Honest caveat
(from the card's own audit):** this partly re-states the collapse-modifies-dynamics vs MWI-only-reinterprets
folklore; the genuine novelty is the closure-package *location* of the touch-point, not the dichotomy itself. The
SBT-native sharpening to attempt (Step 66+): can a **record-stability requirement force the definiteness predicate**
— i.e., is the selection a *down-shadow of the record/memory layer* — and does forcing it require touching **D**
(shape A) or only **Σ_f** (shape B)? Source: `missing_layers/cards/E032.json` (out_of_sample_test).

## Worked instance — Step 69 (record-stability): forbids proton decay + monopoles, *contradicts grand unification*

A concrete Route 5 forbidden co-occurrence that goes **beyond** the GUT-overlapping surface claim — the genuinely new
exclusion is the **link**, not "no decay":

> **A record-bearing (memory-stable) structure is structurally in the clean branch — the proton is *exactly* stable
> (F27 baryon descent = 0) and no monopole exists (F48 = 0) — because the X/Y coset that mediates *both* is excluded by
> record-stability.** The SBT-native, contrarian content is the **link** `record-stability ⟺ baryon conservation + no
> monopole`: SBT predicts the capacity to bear stable records is *structurally tied* to baryon conservation. No other
> framework makes that link; **grand unification predicts the opposite** (a finite proton lifetime + monopoles).

**Falsifier:** observing **proton decay** (e.g. Hyper-Kamiokande) **or a magnetic monopole** — since our universe
manifestly bears stable records, either observation breaks the record-stability⟹clean-separation link and **falsifies
the prediction**. (Distinct from the GUT "long-but-finite lifetime" stance: SBT says *exactly stable / structurally
forbidden* for record-bearing universes.) **Toy basis (manager-verified, symmetric scrutiny):** 0/11,990 record-stable
structures in the decaying branch on the frozen Step-59 carrier; **non-circular** (record-stability ≠ clean-separation as
sets; each memory-stability conjunct alone admits decaying structures — only the conjunction forces clean). **Honest
caveats:** *enumeration-strength* (the all-structures upgrade is lemma **L60→L64**, `PENDING_PROOFS.md`); conditional on
memory-stability as the grounding; *frame-transfer-limited* — **not** a physical proof the proton is stable
(`resolves_physical_proton_stability = false`). Source: Step 69 (`steps/step69_*`); the QM-GR analog is the it-from-qubit
prediction (`thread_qm_gr` Step 55).

## In the framework's own terms (the law-landing bar already names the falsifiable parts)

- **Obligation #2 — independently-checkable consequence** (a forbidden region or a prediction, checkable *without*
  assuming the construction). Routes 2/4/5 discharge this.
- **Obligation #3 — derived closed-form relation** among physical quantities (the `E=mc²` shape). **No track has
  produced this for physics yet** — the SM track produced selections and exclusions, not a *formula*.

A falsifiable claim **is** discharging #2 or #3 **about nature, not the toy.**

## Recommendation — the first real test

Not the SM. Take a **tractable substrate** where the six birds genuinely run, and aim for either:
- **(a) a thermodynamic-consequence bound** (Route 4 — most natural; the machinery already emits it), or
- **(b) a derived scaling relation** (Route 3),

**with the non-definability / forcing argument that it is substrate-forced, not tuned.** One clean, checkable,
substrate-independent relation on a runnable system is worth more than a conditional SM number — and unlike the SM
target, it is actually reachable. The single most physicist-legible milestone: get the strict-extension machinery,
on some runnable substrate, to emit a **closed-form relation among measurable quantities** (obligation #3).
