F34 full-boundary factorization theorem

Scope. Let K be any field, d>0, L in K^(m x d), R in K^(n x d),
and M=L R^T. Assume rank(M)=d. The full readout is the raw,
unnormalized boundary matrix, with fixed boundary bases and fixed internal
dimension. Equality of normalized boundary rays instead requires allowing
an overall scalar; it is not the literal equality used here.

Theorem. For every L',R' of the same sizes with L' R'^T=M, there is a
unique G in GL(d,K) such that L'=L G and R'=R G^(-T). Conversely such
a gauge preserves M. Thus the full-readout fiber is exactly one gauge orbit
on this stratum. Every gauge-invariant function of the pair factors through
the full readout (on its image); no efficiency or regularity is asserted.

Proof. Since rank(M)=d and each factor has d columns, L,R,L',R' all
have full column rank. R^T is onto K^d, hence im(M)=im(L); similarly
im(M)=im(L'). The columns of L and L' are bases of the same d-dimensional
space. There is therefore a unique invertible change-of-basis G with L'=LG.
Choose any left inverse A of L. From LR^T=LG R'^T, left multiplication
by A gives R^T=G R'^T, and hence R'=R G^(-T). The converse follows by
L G (R G^(-T))^T=L G G^(-1) R^T=L R^T. This proves the universal
fiber statement without inferring it from finitely many reconstructed pairs.

Constructive return. Select d independent rows of L and let their square
matrix be U. Then G=U^(-1) L'[selected rows,:]. The Python constructor
implements this return and verifies both intertwiner identities exactly.
The proof is general linear algebra; Python checks exact finite instances,
and is not a Lean mechanization of the universally quantified theorem.

Projective version. If instead the full readout is the normalized boundary
ray, equality means M'=c M for some nonzero scalar c (over R or C). Apply
the theorem to L' and R'/c to obtain L'=L G and R'=c R G^(-T). Thus a
projective full-readout fiber is one orbit only after allowing the additional
overall scalar. Recoverability then applies to functions invariant under
both internal GL and overall scaling. The norm-squared of M, although
internal-GL invariant, does not descend to normalized rays: (2L,R) has the
same ray and a fourfold norm-squared. Step53's raw Gram spectrum likewise
cannot be recovered from a normalized ray without a scale convention.

Rank-deficient control. With d=2 and m=n=2, take
L=diag(1,0), R=I, L'=diag(1,0), R'=diag(1,0).
Both contractions equal diag(1,0), a nonzero boundary state, but rank(R)=2
and rank(R')=1. An invertible internal gauge preserves factor ranks, so
the two pairs are not gauge-related. The full-rank condition is necessary
for this universal conclusion; no conclusion about all degenerate fibers
or general many-vertex tensor networks follows.

Connection to Step 53. Its chosen carrier uses two real tensor frames
of size 81 x 27 and raw contraction LR^T. For seeds 101,202,303, the
new certificate reduces every represented binary-rational frame modulo
the prime 2147483647 and finds rank 27. Nonzero modular minors prove
exact rational column rank 27, so the exact product of those rational
frames has rank 27 and the general theorem applies. This does not
certify the rank of the rounded floating-point matrix product: rounding
can introduce extra small singular values. Historical SVD and residual
rows remain numerical controls rather than proofs of exact rank.

Exact partial-readout witness. For the seed-101 binary-rational factors let U
be the 81-dimensional permutation exchanging coordinates 0 and 1, and replace
R by U R. Then M'=M U^T and M'M'^T=MM^T, so the normalized left reduced
state is identical. For the right observable Z=diag(1,-1,0,...,0), write
q_j=r_j^T(L^T L)r_j for the j-th row r_j of R and N=sum_j q_j>0.
The expectation is (q_0-q_1)/N and changes sign after the swap. The new
certificate evaluates these quantities with exact integer Gram arithmetic
and finds q_0-q_1 nonzero. Thus the two full normalized rays differ while
the left reduced readout agrees. This exact complement-unitary witness
supplies a physical-readout obstruction for the finite toy; the historical
random orthogonal transformation was checked only in floating arithmetic.
Neither this witness nor the full-rank theorem supplies evaporation dynamics
or a theorem about real black holes.
